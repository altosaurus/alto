Lessons

Lessons are step-by-step walks through a topic for someone meeting it for the first time. Nothing about them is scored or recorded.

Finding them

The home page has a switch at the top, Exercises and Lessons. Choose Lessons to see a tile for each lesson, with the icon of the exercise that practises the same topic: the Circle of Fifths lesson has the circle of fifths icon. Alto remembers which side you were on. The cards change with the transition chosen under Settings… › Appearance › Transitions. Lessons in the menu (☰) also goes straight to them.

The switch stays in the top bar inside an exercise or a lesson, as two icons without words. The one for the side you are on is chosen, and pressing either returns to the home page on that side.

A tile says Start, or Continue at step N if you have begun. Click it to open the lesson, and Leave lesson (or Esc) to return to where you were. Start over goes back to the first step.

The Circle of Fifths exercise also has a way in: open its reference pane with the book button, and press New to this? Take the full lesson at the top.

The lesson list

Inside a lesson, the list on the left shows the lessons, as the exercise list does in an exercise. The lesson that is open is in bold, with its steps listed under it and the one you are on highlighted. Click a step to go to it, or another lesson to open that. The button at the top left of the lesson hides and shows the list, and Alto remembers your choice.

Inside a lesson

A lesson is a series of steps. The title and progress are at the top and Back and Next are at the bottom. Back on the first step, and Finish on the last, leave the lesson.

A step has some reading, and may have:

  • A sound to hear. A button plays the notes being described, in the instrument chosen in the settings.
  • The circle of fifths. Click a key, or use the arrow keys, to see its key signature and its chords. Some steps have Down a fifth and Up a fifth buttons that walk round the circle for you.
  • A small exercise. It cannot be failed. A wrong answer gets a hint, a second wrong answer shows the answer with its explanation, and a right answer is explained. Try another asks a new one.

The Circle of Fifths lesson

Eight steps for a beginner:

  1. Why keys need a map. What a key is, and what a key signature is.
  2. A fifth. Counting five letter names, and hearing the interval. A small exercise: name the letter a fifth above.
  3. Walking clockwise. Each step clockwise adds a sharp.
  4. Counting sharps. Find the key with a given number of sharps.
  5. Flats go the other way. Counterclockwise adds flats; where the two meet. Find the key with a given number of flats.
  6. The inner ring: relative minors. Find the relative minor of a key.
  7. A key and its neighbours. The IV and V of a key. Find the key a fifth above or below another.
  8. What you can do now. A summary, and a button to practise in the Circle of Fifths exercise.
