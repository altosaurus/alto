## Keyboard

For keyboard practice in Notation Identification, select **Question Type → Answer Button**. When you choose an exercise from the list, the keyboard goes to it,
so its keys work at once. <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> move between the buttons, and
<kbd>Space</kbd> presses the one that has the focus. Hover over any button to see its key.

### Choosing an answer

| Keys                                                                                       | What it does                                                                                                                             |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>9</kbd>                                                               | Press the matching answer button, counting from the left. Available when an exercise has nine answers or fewer.                          |
| <kbd>C</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> <kbd>G</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> | Keyboard Note Identification and Circle of Fifths: the natural note or key of that name. A minor key is typed as the major key of its letter. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd>+a letter, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+a letter                                        | Keyboard Note Identification and Circle of Fifths: the sharp (<kbd>Shift</kbd>) or the flat (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>) of that note or key.            |
| <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>6</kbd>                                                               | Circle of Fifths, Roman numerals: I, ii, iii, IV, V and vi. For a key with two names (such as B and C♭) press the key of each name.     |
| <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>9</kbd>                                                               | Keyboard Note Identification with the octave asked: the octave, typed as its number.                                                          |
| <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd>                                        | Arpeggio Identification: the inversion, which is the second row of buttons. The chord type is on <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>4</kbd>.           |

With scale-degree answers, digits name the degrees. In a bank with more than nine choices, add <kbd>Shift</kbd> for a sharp
or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> for a flat; add <kbd>Alt</kbd> as well for a double sharp or double flat. The octave row uses
<kbd>A</kbd>, <kbd>S</kbd>, <kbd>D</kbd>, <kbd>F</kbd> and so on, as shown on its buttons.

### Staff placement in Note, Interval, and Scale Construction

Clicking the staff is never needed. A pale note, the **caret**, shows the note that would be placed, and the line under the
staff says what comes next, in words that a screen reader can read.

| Keys                                      | Button              | What it does                                                                                            |
|-------------------------------------------|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| <kbd>↑</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd>               | Higher / Lower      | Move the note one line or space up or down. The first press starts from the middle line of the staff.   |
| <kbd>Page Up</kbd> / <kbd>Page Down</kbd> | Octave ↑ / Octave ↓ | Move the note an octave up or down.                                                                     |
| <kbd>A</kbd> to <kbd>G</kbd>              |                     | Jump to the nearest note with that name, above or below.                                                |
| <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>→</kbd>               |                     | Step backwards or forwards through no sign, ♯, ♭ and ♮ (♮ only when the staff has a key signature). |
| <kbd>+</kbd> <kbd>-</kbd> <kbd>0</kbd>    | ♯ ♭ ♮            | Choose a sharp, a flat or a natural for the next note.                                                  |
| <kbd>Return</kbd>                         | Place note          | Place the note at the caret.                                                                            |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd>                      | Undo                | Scale Construction: take back the last note or step.                                                    |

Interval Construction also accepts <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>-</kbd> for a double flat and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>+</kbd> for a double sharp.

Once all the notes of a scale are placed, and the **Steps** setting is on, the same keys mark the steps:

| Keys                                                | Button    | What it does                                         |
|-----------------------------------------------------|-----------|------------------------------------------------------|
| <kbd>W</kbd> / <kbd>H</kbd>                         | W / H     | Choose a whole or a half step.                       |
| <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> |           | Switch between whole and half.                       |
| <kbd>Return</kbd>                                   | Mark step | Mark the step and move on to the next pair of notes. |

When everything is placed, <kbd>Return</kbd> presses **Answer**. <kbd>Return</kbd> always does the next thing: it places a
note or marks a step while there is something to place, and answers once there is not.

### Building a key signature (Key Signature Construction)

| Keys                         | What it does                                  |
|------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| <kbd>↑</kbd> or <kbd>→</kbd> | One more sharp (or one fewer flat).           |
| <kbd>↓</kbd> or <kbd>←</kbd> | One more flat (or one fewer sharp).           |
| <kbd>Home</kbd>              | Back to the natural sign: no sharps or flats. |
| <kbd>Return</kbd>            | Answer.                                       |

### Placing a clef (Clef Basics, Clef Construction)

| Keys                         | What it does                                        |
|------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| <kbd>←</kbd> or <kbd>→</kbd> | Choose the G, C or F clef before or after this one. |
| <kbd>↑</kbd> or <kbd>↓</kbd> | Move it up or down a line or space.                 |
| <kbd>Return</kbd>            | Answer: place it where it is.                       |

### Everywhere

| Keys                                        | What it does                                  |
|---------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| <kbd>Esc</kbd>                              | Go back from the statistics or database page. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd> | Open the app settings.                        |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd>                | Restart Alto.                                 |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Q</kbd>                | Quit.                                         |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>+</kbd>                | Zoom in. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>=</kbd> does the same, so no <kbd>Shift</kbd> is needed. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>-</kbd>                | Zoom out.                                     |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>0</kbd>                | Go back to 100%.                              |

On macOS, Qt shows these shortcuts with <kbd>⌘</kbd> in place of <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>.

Zoom makes everything larger or smaller together: the text, the buttons, the lists, the notation, the keyboard and the icons. It
moves through 50%, 67%, 75%, 80%, 90%, 100%, 110%, 125%, 150%, 175%, 200%, 250% and 300%. Alto draws the window again at each
step, and remembers the zoom the next time it starts. The current percentage briefly appears to the left of the Glossary button. Windows that are already open, such as the settings, take the new
zoom when you open them again.

## Mouse

| Action                        | Where                                                        | What it does                                                                             |
|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Click the staff               | Note, Interval, and Scale Construction                        | Place the note.                                                                          |
| Right-click the staff         | Note Construction, Scale Construction                         | Step through no sign, sharp, flat and natural.                                           |
| Turn the wheel over the staff | Scale Construction                                           | Step through the signs while notes are placed, then switch between whole and half steps. |
| Double-click a row            | Database: Answers or Questions                               | Preview that question.                                                                   |
| Click a column heading        | Database                                                     | Sort by that column.                                                                     |
| Hover a chart                 | Statistics                                                   | Show the exact values for that period.                                                   |
| Turn the wheel with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> held | Anywhere                                        | Zoom in or out one step for each click of the wheel.                                     |
| Drag the edge of the sidebar  | Exercise screens                                             | Resize the exercise list, or drag it narrower than its minimum to hide it.               |

## The menu

Open the ☰ menu at the top left for **Profile**, **Settings…**, **Tools**, **Help**, **Restart**, or **Quit**.
Under **Help**, **About** shows the installed version and development status. **Report an issue** opens Alto’s GitHub issues page.
Under **Tools**, choose **Import backup…**, **Export backup…**, **Anki Exporter**, or **Check for updates…**.
For deck selection, scheduling, and import instructions, see [Anki decks]({{ '/data/' | relative_url }}#anki-decks).
