Lessons are step-by-step walks through a topic for someone meeting it for the first time. Nothing about them is scored or recorded.

Start with **Pitches and Notes** to connect a sound to a piano key and a written note. Its last step opens
**Keyboard Note Identification**, **Note Identification**, **Note Construction**, or **Notation Identification**. Each exercise’s book button
opens hints for that exercise and a link back to the lesson. **Clef Basics** then explains how to read different clefs.
**Scale Identification** uses that notation to explore patterns of notes. **The Circle of Fifths** shows how keys and
their signatures relate.

When you open an exercise from a lesson, **Back to lesson** at the upper left of the exercise returns you to the step you left.

## Finding them

The home page has a switch at the top, **Exercises** and **Lessons**. Choose **Lessons** to see a tile for each lesson, with the
icon of the exercise that practices the same topic: the Circle of Fifths lesson has the circle of fifths icon. Alto remembers which
side you were on. The cards change with the transition chosen under **Settings… › Appearance › Transitions**. **Lessons** in the menu (☰) also goes straight to them.

The switch stays in the top bar inside an exercise or a lesson, as two icons without words. The one for the side you are on is
chosen, and pressing either returns to the home page on that side.

A tile says **Start**, or **Continue at step N** if you have begun. Click it to open the lesson, and **Leave lesson** (or <kbd>Esc</kbd>)
to return to where you were. **Start over** goes back to the first step.

The Circle of Fifths exercise also has a way in: open its reference pane with the book button, and press **New to this? Take the
full lesson** at the top. The Clef Basics exercise has the same, with a table of the clefs under the button.

## The lesson list

Inside a lesson, the list on the left shows the lessons, as the exercise list does in an exercise. The lesson that is open is in bold,
with its steps listed under it and the one you are on highlighted. Click a step to go to it, or another lesson to open that. The
button at the top left of the lesson hides and shows the list, and Alto remembers your choice.

## Inside a lesson

A lesson is a series of steps. The title and progress are at the top and **Back** and **Next** are at the bottom. **Back** on the first
step, and **Finish** on the last, leave the lesson.

A step has some reading, and may have:

- **A sound to hear.** A button plays the notes being described, in the instrument chosen in the settings.
- **A keyboard.** In Pitches and Notes, a highlighted piano key shows where a pitch falls among its neighbors.
- **Staves.** The clef lesson shows staves beside the reading, each with a clef, sometimes a note, and a line under it. In a
  small exercise the staff changes with the question.
- **The circle of fifths.** Click a key, or use the arrow keys, to see its key signature and its chords. Some steps have **Down a
  fifth** and **Up a fifth** buttons that walk around the circle for you.
- **A small exercise.** It cannot be failed. A wrong answer gets a hint, a second wrong answer shows the answer with its
  explanation, and a right answer is explained. **Try another** asks a new one.

## The Circle of Fifths lesson

Eight steps for a beginner:

1. **Why keys need a map.** What a key is, and what a key signature is.
2. **A fifth.** Counting five letter names, and hearing the interval. A small exercise: name the letter a fifth above.
3. **Walking clockwise.** Each step clockwise adds a sharp.
4. **Counting sharps.** Find the key with a given number of sharps.
5. **Flats go the other way.** Counterclockwise adds flats; where the two meet. Find the key with a given number of flats.
6. **The inner ring: relative minors.** Find the relative minor of a key.
7. **A key and its neighbors.** The IV and V of a key. Find the key a fifth above or below another.
8. **What you can do now.** A summary, and a button to practice in the Circle of Fifths exercise.

## The Clef Basics lesson

Ten steps for a beginner:

1. **What a clef does.** The same place on the staff is a different note in the treble and bass clefs.
2. **Three symbols.** Every clef is the G clef, the C clef or the F clef, put on a line.
3. **The G clef.** The treble clef, and the French violin clef on the bottom line.
4. **The F clef.** The bass clef on the fourth line, the subbass clef on the top line, and the baritone clef.
5. **The C clef and middle C.** Soprano, mezzo-soprano, alto and tenor are the C clef on four lines, and each centers on middle C.
6. **The rarer clefs.** French violin, subbass and the two ways to write the baritone clef.
7. **Name the symbol.** A clef is drawn: is it the G, C or F clef?
8. **Find the line.** A named clef: which line of a blank staff is it written on?
9. **Name the clef.** The two halves together, as in Clef Identification.
10. **What you can do now.** A summary, and a button to practice in the Clef Basics exercise.
